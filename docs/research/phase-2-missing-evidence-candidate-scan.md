# Phase 2 missing-evidence question leads

**Purpose:** source-first leads for possible Phase 2 question design, based on the accepted 100-paper snapshot. These are candidate topics for checking whether the reviewed literature reports a particular evaluation; they are not claims that the topic is absent from all research, relevance labels, evidence anchors, or benchmark assignments.

## Bounded scope

The accepted membership and version metadata were checked in `manifests/phase1-100-paper-shortlist-proposal.json` and `local-reference/phase1-100/pdf-source-verification.json` (snapshot `4b11fab3-d4a5-4e7a-a58e-8654accf2c6c`). Each cited accepted local PDF has a valid recorded SHA-256. The source scan screened titles and available OpenAlex abstracts across the 100 accepted items for prompt injection/security, corpus updates/freshness, and calibration/abstention terms, then reviewed the listed near-match PDFs through their official venue or author repository full text. It did not run a full-text search over every accepted PDF. Therefore these are bounded candidate gaps, not corpus-wide absence adjudications. No retrieval-result data was consulted.

## Candidate topics

1. **Instruction-bearing prompt injection in retrieved documents.** Candidate question: *Do any accepted RAG evaluations insert malicious instructions into retrieved passages and test whether the model follows or resists them, separately from false facts, irrelevant noise, and conflicts with model knowledge?* Near-matches: ReEval (`W4401042876`, `publishedVersion`) perturbs evidence by answer swapping and context enrichment; its evaluation tests whether models use altered factual evidence ([ACL paper, §§1 and 3](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.85.pdf)). Astute RAG (`W4412889733`, `publishedVersion`) studies irrelevant, misleading, or malicious retrieval and conflicts with parametric knowledge, but its stated setup is reliability-aware consolidation and answering rather than an instruction-injection attack ([ACL paper, abstract and §1](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1476.pdf)). The official full texts contain no exact `prompt injection` match. This is a possible distinction to verify against other accepted papers, not a claim that malicious retrieval is unstudied.

2. **Freshness of an already-built persistent index after source changes.** Candidate question: *When a source document is revised or removed after indexing, do accepted studies test whether a persistent retrieval index is refreshed and stale evidence stops influencing the answer?* ReEval (`W4401042876`, `publishedVersion`) edits evidence supplied to the model and evaluates the generated answer; it does not describe maintaining a persistent retrieval index ([ACL paper, Figure 1 and §§3.1–3.2](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-naacl.85.pdf)). RadioRAG (`W4402853403`, selected `submittedVersion`) is a positive near-match for freshness: it retrieves current online material, then builds an on-the-fly vector database for each query ([accepted arXiv PDF, abstract and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.15621); [publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1148/ryai.240476)). That per-query database does not by itself test refresh or deletion behavior in a persistent index. Keep the candidate narrowly about post-index source changes.

3. **Calibrated abstention when retrieved evidence is insufficient.** Candidate question: *For unanswerable RAG queries, do accepted studies calibrate a refusal threshold against evidence sufficiency and report a reliability or risk–coverage trade-off?* QA-RAG (`W4402371950`, `publishedVersion`) evaluates 500 questions whose answer is missing from its knowledge base and reports the exact-match rate for rejecting them; this is an abstention-adjacent positive case, but the described result is not a calibrated threshold or risk–coverage curve ([MDPI paper, §3.5 and §4.3](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-2289/8/9/115)). FLARE (`W4389519118`, `publishedVersion`) uses low token probability to trigger retrieval, rather than to refuse an answer ([ACL paper, §3.2](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.495.pdf)). Astute RAG (`W4412889733`) does assign confidence to candidate answers and evaluates whether it aligns with supporting-context confidence ([ACL paper, Appendix B](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1476.pdf)); any eventual question must distinguish that confidence assessment from calibrated selective answering. This lead is the most sensitive to additional full-text review.
